THE POST:

A stock once looked six times cheaper than reality — in our own product.

Nobody typed a wrong number. The data feed delivered a Danish company's figures in kroner, and our pipeline displayed them with a dollar sign. Kroner are worth about 15 cents. Every ratio built on that number was confidently, precisely wrong.

Here's what makes this class of error dangerous: it doesn't look like corruption. It looks like a bargain.

And currency mixing has a sneakier sibling — period mixing. Revenue from last fiscal year, profit from the trailing twelve months, share count from last quarter: every ratio crosses time periods, wrong by a little, in directions you can't see.

So every analysis card in AI Stock Assist now carries two stamps: "Data as of" — one date, one filing period for every number — and, for foreign stocks, the exact conversion rate with its date, printed on the card. Behind them, an enforcement layer recomputes the displayed ratios from the displayed figures before anything renders. A card that disagrees with itself doesn't render.

We kept the broken data as a canary in our test suite. Every future change must still catch it, or it doesn't ship.

The card does the arithmetic; you make the decision.

Post 1 of "The App That Shows Its Work" is live (link below).
