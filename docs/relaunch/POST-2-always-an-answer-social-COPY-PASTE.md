THE POST:

One July morning, our product decided ExxonMobil was unratable.

Not risky. Not overvalued. Unratable — NOT RATED, no further explanation. Same for Chevron. Same for Merck. Ten of the most heavily analyzed companies on earth, and our system shrugged at most of them.

The companies were fine. Our data vendor was rate-limiting us and fields were arriving empty. Our system did what a lot of analytical systems do with missing inputs: it refused to answer. Which sounds prudent — until you're the user who paid for an analysis and received silence.

There are two wrong responses to missing data. Silence is the first. The second is worse: bluffing — filling gaps with zeros and scoring as if nothing happened, so a missing debt figure becomes "no debt" and the verdict comes out confident and hollow.

We built the third option. Score what verified. Name every gap, on the card, above the verdict ("ROE: unavailable from the data source"). Lower the stated confidence so you know exactly how much weight the answer can bear. NOT RATED still exists — but it's reserved for data we can't trust, not data we don't have.

Missing data is never bad data. And it's never silence either.

The card does the arithmetic; you make the decision.
