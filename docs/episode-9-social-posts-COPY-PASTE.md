THE POST:

"After ten years of picking stocks on instinct… how do you actually know your instinct is any good?"

I watched that question land on someone last week. He's a smart, experienced investor. He buys on feel — reads about a company, checks the numbers that day, gets a gut sense, pulls the trigger. "I've done fine," he said.

Then came the follow-up: the last stock you sold at a loss — why did you buy it in the first place? What was the business quality the day you bought?

He couldn't say. He doesn't keep a record.

Here's the hard truth: picking by feel for ten years doesn't give you ten years of experience. It gives you one year, ten times — because nothing was ever written down to learn from. A decision you can't audit can't make you better.

This is the part of AI Stock Assist almost nobody asks about, and it might be the most valuable: every analysis you run is saved, dated, and reopenable. A casebook. Click any past analysis and the whole verdict opens exactly as it was that day — what you concluded, and why.

It's the difference between two detectives. One trusts his gut and stays exactly as good as he was on day one. The other keeps a notebook, rereads her own old cases, and gets sharper every year — because she can finally tell which hunches were real and which were noise.

You don't have to be a genius investor. You just have to be willing to read your own old cases.
